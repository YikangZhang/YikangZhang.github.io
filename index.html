About

Psychology, crime and the law

My work sits between social psychology, criminology and law. I study how peers, groups and individual differences shape memory, the gathering of information, and unethical behavior in legal and institutional settings.

I use experiments, longitudinal and cross-cultural surveys, pooled analyses of raw data across studies, and virtual-reality experiments in which participants make decisions alongside their peers. I co-lead the Partners in Crime program at the Max Planck Institute, run a program on how schools investigate student misconduct, and co-supervise four PhD researchers.

I received my PhD in legal psychology from Maastricht University in 2024, after training in applied psychology at East China Normal University. I work in English and Mandarin.
